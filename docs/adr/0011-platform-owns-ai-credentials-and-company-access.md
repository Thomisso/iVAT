# Platform owns AI credentials and Company access

AI provider configuration and credentials are platform know-how managed only by the SuperAdmin. The SuperAdmin creates named AI Configurations containing the provider credential and the models used for Split and Extraction; one configuration may be assigned to one Company or shared by several Companies without duplicating its secret. The SuperAdmin assigns configurations, grants AI access and sets each Company's AI Processing Allowance, while Organization Administrators, Company Administrators and Users have no AI configuration or BYOK controls.

A new Company receives the shared default AI Configuration, AI access and an allowance of 50 successful Extractions unless the SuperAdmin overrides them. The allowance is a lifetime total and does not reset automatically; failed attempts, Split and technical retries do not consume it, while a successful repeated Extraction does. When a Source File contains more Documents than the remaining allowance, Documents are processed in page order up to the limit and the rest wait for allowance; increasing the limit automatically requeues the oldest waiting Documents. The SuperAdmin may grant unlimited processing. Company-managed credentials may be reconsidered later, but are outside the current product scope.

Expected platform interventions are distinct from processing failures. Documents wait without retrying when their Company has exhausted its allowance or lacks a usable AI Configuration; transient provider failures retry automatically and become failed only after the retry budget is exhausted. Users see platform-neutral explanations, while the SuperAdmin sees provider and credential details.

Platform Administration is the operational control surface for this model. Its active dashboard groups allowance waits, configuration and provider incidents, and terminal processing failures by root cause, AI Configuration and Company; it warns when a Company reaches 80% of its allowance and offers the corresponding remediation in place. Shared-configuration incidents show every affected Company without exposing the credential secret. Resolved conditions leave the active dashboard automatically, while SuperAdmin interventions remain in the Audit Log. The active dashboard remains the authority for operational problems. SuperAdmins can additionally opt in to operational email on their own current verified account address, with alerts disabled by default. The background worker sends a grouped first alert only after five minutes of continuously observed critical work blockage; shared-root impact changes preserve the episode. Transient warnings, 80% allowance warnings and exhausted allowance without waiting work remain in-app. Source Files, Documents and export jobs retain their own impact units.

The email channel keeps private, durable episode and recipient/outbox state, without introducing incident-history UI. Resolutions observed by database transitions invalidate an episode even between worker ticks. Worker-only reconciliation and atomic claims use the same private authoritative readers as the MFA-protected public platform queries. Every dispatch checks current SuperAdmin Role, active Auth account, opt-in and current verified address. Mail contains localized problem labels, counts and a canonical protected remediation link; names, financial content, credentials and raw provider responses are omitted.

SMTP acceptance, refusal, definite local failure and unknown outcomes remain distinct. Claims interrupted by process loss become unknown and are never automatically reclaimed; neither SMTP acceptance nor durable deduplication guarantees Inbox or exactly-once delivery. Each receipt gets one attempt, with no automatic transport retries. Only a recipient's accepted first alert qualifies that recipient for reminders and recovery. The first reminder waits at least 24 elapsed hours after first acceptance; every later reminder waits at least 24 hours after the last actual first/reminder dispatch attempt, including refused or failed attempts. Downtime creates one current opportunity, without a catch-up burst. An unknown reminder suppresses later reminders while its outcome remains uncertain.

Complete remediation closes the episode and cancels pending first alerts and reminders before the public mutation commits. A shared root stays active until its final blocked work is remediated. Reconciliation plans one recovery receipt per qualified recipient and resolved episode, including a first acceptance recorded after resolution. Recovery rechecks current recipient eligibility and describes that episode ending, without promising that a later recurrence is healthy. A genuinely new blockage gets a new episode and the full five-minute observation grace; impact changes preserve the existing identity and dispatch clock. Token-based outcome comparison accepts a definitive late result only for its original in-flight receipt. The existing active dashboard and Audit Log remain the operational authorities. Email failures never become new operational incidents. A stopped worker cannot report its own total outage.

Organization-level AI settings and usage views are removed. The SuperAdmin sees full usage, token, allowance and waiting-Document data by AI Configuration and Company. A Company Administrator sees only the Company's successful Extractions against its allowance and the number of waiting Documents; a User sees only the status of their processing and platform-neutral errors.

## Considered Options

- Organization BYOK and Administrator-managed AI settings were removed from the current product to keep provider selection, credential management and commercial limits under platform control.
